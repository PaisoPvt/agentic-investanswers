## Goal
BTCUSD and ETHUSD get daily, weekly and monthly bars built the way TradingView builds them (day 00:00 to 24:00 UTC, week from Monday 00:00 UTC, month from the 1st 00:00 UTC). They are cooked into the store, read as settled bars plus a tail, checked by the rebuild check and source fidelity, and served by the History API and live to charts.

## Plan
What I found:
- **Build parity already passes:** TradingView's crypto 1D, 1W and 1M exports rolled up by our `rollUp` are exact (1h→1D, 1D→1W and 1M). So the bucketing is right; what is missing is everything around it.
- **Where 1D, 1W and 1M are refused today**, all marked "M1-08":
  - `CookIntraday` (throws on them);
  - the cooked reader (reads them from the store only);
  - `whyNotServed` (refuses them);
  - the rebuild check, `pnpm cook`, live cooking and the cache list (leave them out).
- **Storage:** calendar timeframes already have yearly partitions (`cooked/<symbol>/1d/<YYYY>.parquet`).
- **`rollUp` takes any ascending bars,** and build parity already rolls 1D into 1W and 1M with it. For crypto a UTC day never crosses a week or month start, so 1W and 1M rolled from 1D equal the same bars rolled from minutes, and cost a year file instead of up to 31 days of minutes.

Steps:
1. **Cooking** (`contexts/market-data`, `CookIntraday`):
   - 1D is rolled up from the same minutes that already build the intraday timeframes, both in `cookPartition` (`pnpm cook`, backfill, gap repair) and in `cookRangeAll` (the compactor, per settled day).
   - Then the 1W and 1M bars that contain those days are rebuilt from the stored 1D bars. A week that straddles a new year is read across both year files.
   - Crypto only: stocks keep refusing calendar timeframes until M1-08b.
2. **Reading** (`CookedReader`):
   - 1D: stored bars before the watermark, plus today's bar rolled up from its minutes, like intraday.
   - 1W and 1M: stored bars before the bar that contains the watermark, plus a tail rolled up from 1D (settled 1D bars plus today's).
   So a 1M request reads the year file and today's minutes, never 31 days of minutes.
3. **Live** (`LiveCook`, `FormingBars`, the pipeline):
   - each stored minute also rebuilds today's 1D bar, from today's minutes held in memory (read once at start), and the forming 1W and 1M, from the week's and month's settled 1D bars, read once per day and refreshed after compaction, plus today's 1D;
   - forming trades update them as they do 4h;
   - the recent-bars cache and the live gateway carry 1D, 1W and 1M.
4. **Served:** `whyNotServed` allows crypto 1D, 1W and 1M; stocks stay refused (M1-08b). The chart then shows the D/W/M buttons on its own, from `/instruments`.
5. **Checks:**
   - the rebuild check compares settled 1D bars per month, rebuilt from that month's minutes, and settled 1W and 1M bars rebuilt from the rebuilt 1D;
   - source fidelity reports stored 1D, 1W and 1M against the exports (reported only, as for the others);
   - `pnpm cook` and `pnpm rebuild-check` stop saying "M1-08".
6. **Tests:**
   - cooking: a week across a year end, a month end, partial days;
   - the reader's tail for 1W and 1M across the watermark;
   - live forming of 1D, 1W and 1M across midnight, a week start and a month start;
   - served;
   - the rebuild check catching a wrong stored 1D, 1W or 1M;
   - e2e: the e2e server rolls 1D, 1W and 1M too, and the chart switches to 1D and 1W and gets a live update.
7. **Data:**
   - locally: `pnpm cook` from 2024-11, then `pnpm rebuild-check`, then `pnpm parity`, all green;
   - on AWS, after deploy: a one-off cook task.
8. **Docs:** architecture (calendar timeframes: cooking, reading, live), bar-rules (crypto D/W/M built and checked), card handoff.

**One thing to know:** local and AWS raw crypto history starts in 2024-11, so charts get about two years of 1D, 1W and 1M. TradingView's exports go back to 2014 (BTC) and 2016 (ETH). Matching that depth means backfilling about ten years of 1-minute bars per instrument from Coinbase (several hours per instrument, and S3 storage). That's a separate decision, not part of this task; parity is unaffected.
